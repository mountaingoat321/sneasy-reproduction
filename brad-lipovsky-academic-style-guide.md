# Academic writing guidance

Original project summary of the guidance read at
[Brad Lipovsky's academic style guide](https://github.com/bradlipovsky/vdv-damage/blob/main/brad-lipovsky-academic-style-guide.md).
Consult that source for the full guide. Its text is not included here because
redistribution permission has not been established.

Begin with the physical question and the result that the calculation supports.
Explain why each equation is needed. Define geometry, assumptions, units,
initial conditions, and boundary conditions before interpreting solutions.
Use quantitative comparisons and restrained language. Distinguish numerical
verification from agreement with observations, and agreement from validation
of an extrapolation. Place citations next to the claims they support.

Organize results around informative figures. Captions must identify the data,
units, statistics, parameter choices, and meaning of graphical marks. Describe
limitations concretely, including the direction of a bias when it is known.
Avoid claims of discovery, equivalence, or successful reproduction that exceed
the evidence. Finish with a bounded implication and a testable next step.
